Abstract

We study reinforcement learning for a contact-rich robot manipulation task in which a Franka Panda arm with a paddle must keep a ping-pong ball airborne through repeated bouncing. For the midterm milestone, we completed experiments with one on-policy policy-gradient method, PPO, and one off-policy Q-function-based method, SAC. We compare both methods against a random-agent baseline on the same task using mean episodic return versus training environment steps. Our current results show that PPO learns useful behavior earlier in training, while SAC improves more slowly at first but appears to converge to a higher final reward. We also analyze how PPO performance changes as the number of parallel training environments increases, and observe that intermediate-scale parallelism currently works best in our setup. Finally, we outline two modifications for the final project: multi-task training over environment variants and methods for scaling policy learning under very large parallel rollouts.
